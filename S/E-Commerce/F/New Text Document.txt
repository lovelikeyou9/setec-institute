History company of Stripe
Stripe, Inc. is an American financial technology company founded by brothers Patrick Collison and John Collison.

1.1🤔
How Stripe process
2009: Patrick and John Collison started working on what became Stripe.

2010: Stripe was incorporated and began developing its payment infrastructure.

2011: Stripe launched publicly, initially focusing on making online payments easier for developers.

2013: Stripe expanded its payment services and introduced more tools for online businesses.

2016–2019: Expanded internationally and added more payment, billing, fraud-prevention, and business tools.

2020s: Stripe became a major global payments/financial infrastructure company serving businesses around the world.

If you mean what the two Stripe keys are for:
1. pk_test_... — Publishable key
    - Used on the frontend
    - Helps your website communicate with Stripe
    - Can be exposed publicly
2. sk_test_... — Secret key
    - Used on your backend/server

    - Must be kept secret

[ pk_live_... ← frontend ]
[ sk_live_... ← backend ]
is have my money?
No. pk_live_... and sk_live_... do not themselves contain your money.

They are live API keys that allow your application to communicate with your real Stripe account.

If a customer pays you $50:
Customer
   ↓
Visa card
   ↓
Stripe Live
   ↓
$50 payment
   ↓
Your Stripe balance
   ↓
Payout to your bank account
So:
🔑 Live keys = access/authorization credentials
💰 Stripe balance = where payment funds are held before payout
🏦 Bank account = where Stripe can send your payout

bank will send money to them?

If you mean “Does the bank send the customer's money to Stripe?” — yes, generally the payment flow is like this:
Customer
   ↓
Customer's bank
   ↓
Visa network
   ↓
Stripe
   ↓
Your Stripe balance
   ↓
Your bank account

For example, a customer pays $50 with a Visa card:
  1. Customer's bank authorizes the $50 payment.
  2. The card payment is processed through the card network.
  3. Stripe receives/processes the payment funds.
  4. Stripe records the funds in your Stripe balance, minus applicable fees.
  5. Stripe later pays out the available funds to your connected bank account.
